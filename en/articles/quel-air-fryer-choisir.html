Which air fryer to choose? The simple buying guide to avoid paying too much

Capacity, power, cleaning, drawer or double tray, realistic budget: the simple guide to choosing a useful air fryer without paying too much.

Photo: Unsplash

Buying a fryer air is typically the kind of expense that can be either very malinous or completely useless. On paper, it promises quick cooking, less fat and more practicality. In fact, many people buy a model too small, too gadget or too hard to clean, then let it take the dust.

Good air fryer is not the one with the most programs. It's the one that matches your real use. If you're cooking for a person, you don't need a huge machine. If you're three or four at home, taking a "no-cost" mini model is often a mistake: you're going to have to do several batches, waste time and get tired.

What to look for before buying

The first criterion, this is the real capacity. For one person or occasional use, a compact format may be sufficient. For a couple or a family, you have to aim bigger, otherwise the interest quickly disappears. The second criterion, it’s the ease of cleaning. A device that you hate to wash rarely ends up being used for a long time.

The third point, this is the format. A simple drawer air fryer is often enough to get started. A double tray model becomes interesting if you want to cook two foods at the same time or manage several portions without organizing yourself like a team leader.

Power matters too, but less than the marketing suggests. A well-designed, simple and consistent model in its format is often better than a smart device that doesn't really improve daily meals.

What budget is really reasonable?

  • Entry level (~40-60€) : sufficient to test the use and prepare simple portions. Well, if you live alone or want to see if the format suits you.
  • Mid-range (~70-120€) : the best utility/price ratio. Capacité plus confortable, cuisson plus homogène, nettoyage plus simple. Example: Cosori Pro II 5.5L or Tefal Easy Fry Dual 8L.
  • High-end (130€+) : makes sense if you cook often, whether you want a large volume or a well thought out double compartment. But many people pay for design or brand. Example: Philips Airfryer XXL Combi 7.2L or Ninja Double Basket 9.5L.

The real question is not "what's the best fryer?" but: From how many uses does it become profitable for me?

Classic mistakes

The first mistake is buying too small. The second is to buy too sophisticated to heat nuggets twice a week. The third is to forget the congestion. A huge fryer air that lives at the bottom of a closet quickly becomes a bad purchase.

Another trap: believe that "more programs" = better device. In practice, most users almost always return to the same settings.

My EuroMalin advice

If you're starting out, aim for a simple model, easy to wash, with a consistent capacity for your household. Don't pay for gadgets. Pays for convenience. And always compare the final price with cashback before ordering.

  • For those who want to cook faster
  • For small households or couples
  • For those who want to limit the oven for small quantities
  • For those who like simple, often-used devices
